# P83 B1 derivatives — review notes

These are the forms included in the first pass that are correct but uncommon,
or for which a second opinion would help. Nothing here is known to be wrong; the
task rule is that a doubtful form is left out, so the entries below were kept
only because a dictionary or a productive pattern supports them.

## English

- `en:unfulfilled:adjective` → `unfulfillment` (noun). Rare, but formed
  regularly from `unfulfilled`; a reviewer may prefer to drop it.
- `en:enable:verb` → `enablement` (noun). Attested in business/technical use.
- `en:compostable:adjective` → `compostability`; `en:renewable:adjective` →
  `renewability`; `en:approachable:adjective` → `approachability`;
  `en:challenging:adjective` → `challengingly`; `en:sophisticated:adjective` →
  `sophisticatedly`; `en:observant:adjective` → `observantly`. Low-frequency
  derivatives, kept for completeness of the family.
- `en:literal:adjective` → `literalism`; `en:definite:adjective` →
  `definiteness`; `en:diverse:adjective` → `diversely`. Correct but bookish.
- `en:vital:adjective` → `vitally`. Adverbial use is standard, though less
  common than `vitality`.

## Russian

- `ru:deli:noun` → `делийский` (adjective). Regular from `Дели`, but the
  conventional adjective is uncommon.
- `ru:pokolenie:noun` → `поколенческий`. Attested, but `поколенческий` is
  journalistic; some dictionaries prefer a phrase.
- `ru:rekruter:noun` → `рекрутинг`. Borrowed noun, common in HR usage but not in
  every dictionary.

## Greek

- `el:kariera:noun` → `καριερίστας`. Common colloquial noun; standard dictionaries
  differ on register.
- `el:sygkroysi:noun` → `συγκρουσιακός`. Regular adjective, mostly used in
  political/social contexts.
- `el:eisodima:noun` → `εισοδηματικός`. Regular adjective, mainly in economics.
- `el:exoythenosi:noun` → `εξουθενωμένος`. The participle is more common than a
  finite passive; kept as the adjectival family member.

## Italian

- `it:usanza:noun` → `usualmente` (adverb). This was first written
  `usualemente` and corrected; the accepted form is `usualmente`.
- `it:transazione:noun` → `transazionale`; `it:pensione:noun` →
  `pensionistico`; `it:laboratorio:noun` → `laboratoriale`. Regular, but
  specialist vocabulary.
- `it:saggio:noun` → `saggista`. The noun sense here is "essay", so the family
  member is the person, not a derived abstract noun.

## French

- `fr:bretagne:noun` → `breton` (adjective), `Breton` (noun). The capitalised
  noun is the inhabitant; the lowercase adjective follows French usage.
- `fr:bnvole:noun` → `bénévolat`, `bénévolement`. The headword id lost its
  accents upstream; the derivatives use the correct spelling.

## B2 review notes

### English

- English B2 links were proposed from WordNet derivational relations. The
  following were removed by hand because they are synonyms or unrelated words,
  not family members: `dismissal` → `fire`, `sack`; `contend` → `contest`;
  `monetary` → `money`; `revolution` → `revolt`, `revolve`; `underline` →
  `underscore`; `standpoint` → `stand`; `signify` → `sign`.
- Rare but attested forms kept: `compliancy`, `assertable`, `accommodator`,
  `arbitrament`, `vividness`, `enormousness`. A reviewer may prefer to drop the
  rarest of these.
- `en:enormous:adjective` → `enormity` carries `meaning_shift`
  ("a very evil act"), because the modern sense is not "large size".
- 386 English B2 entries still have no field. Most are nouns with no
  single-word family (`firewall`, `gpa`, `spectrum`) or verbs with no derived
  noun (`comprise`, `overlook`, `outweigh`). A later manual pass should decide
  whether to add a family member from outside the current lexicon.
- Adverbs were added only when WordNet contains the adverb. This avoids
  non-words such as `*doctoral+ly` patterns that a spelling rule would create.

### C1 review notes

#### English

- 501 of 879 single-word C1 open-class entries have a family, from 791 WordNet
  links. The filter required a matching part of speech, the candidate to start
  with the first five letters of the headword, and the candidate not to be in
  the same synset as the headword. This removes near-synonyms such as
  `desirable` → `suitableness` but still leaves some low-frequency forms.
- Both spelling variants are kept when WordNet has both, for example
  `normalisation`/`normalization`, `centralise`/`centralize` and
  `subsidisation`/`subsidization`. A later pass may choose one variant per
  entry.
- Some links are base nouns rather than suffixed derivatives: `prestige` from
  `prestigious`, `moment` from `momentary`, `planet` from `planetary`,
  `episteme` from `epistemic`. They are still the same word family.
- `en:practices:noun` is a plural headword; WordNet resolves it to `practice`,
  so the family member is `practical`. A reviewer may prefer to move the field
  to a singular headword if one is added.
- 378 English C1 entries still have no field. Most are concrete nouns,
  loanwords, or words whose only WordNet relatives failed the prefix test.

#### French

- Left out: `invite` (AI prompt; the ordinary verb `inviter` is unrelated),
  `itinéraire` (no confident single-word family) and `infrastructure`
  (`infrastructurel` is specialist).
- `utilitaire` is listed for `utilitarisme` with the everyday sense
  "practical" rather than a strict philosophical counterpart.
- `hameçon` is a base noun ("hook, bait"), not a suffixed derivative of
  `hameçonnage`.

#### Italian

- Left out: `chatbot`, `prompt`, `phishing`, `malware`, `firewall` (loanwords),
  `itinerario` (`itinerante` has a different sense).
- `crittografo`, `climatizzatore` and `arredatore` are agent/instrument nouns,
  not abstract counterparts.

#### Russian

- Left out: `промпт`, `фишинг`, `брандмауэр` (loanwords) and
  `здравоохранение` (no confident single-word family).
- `шифр` is a base noun ("code, cipher"), not a suffixed derivative of
  `шифрование`.

#### Greek

- Left out: `διαδρομή` (no confident single-word family).
- `τηλεργαζόμενος` is an agent noun ("remote worker"), not an abstract
  counterpart of `τηλεργασία`.
- `αυτοματοποιώ`, `κρυπτογραφώ`, `διακοσμώ` and the philosophy adjectives are
  regular derivations; the corresponding nouns in -ση/-ία are the headwords.

### C2 review notes

Only English, French, Italian, Russian and Greek are in scope. The other
repository languages are not part of this pass.

#### English

- 365 of 618 single-word C2 open-class entries have a family, from 556 WordNet
  links. The same filter as C1 applies: matching part of speech, five-letter
  prefix, and no same-synset synonym.
- The 253 English C2 entries without a field are mostly nouns and adjectives
  whose WordNet relatives failed the prefix test, or where the only relative is
  a near-synonym.
- Low-frequency forms were kept when WordNet lists them, for example
  `syllogise`/`syllogize` and `comprehendible`.

#### French

- 37 of 124 entries annotated, one derived noun per verb.
- Left out when the noun is irregular or not transparent: `nier` (`négation`),
  `militer` (`militantisme`), `vicier` (`viciation`), and verbs whose usual noun
  is a phrase.
- `acquiescement` is standard but less common than `consentement`.
  `médiation` is listed for the rare verb `médier`.

#### Italian

- 35 of 121 entries annotated, one derived noun per verb.
- Left out: `viziare` (`vizio` is a base noun), `acconsentire`
  (`acconsentimento` is rare beside `consenso`), `racchiudere`
  (`racchiusura` is uncommon), and `ingenerare`.
- `recupero` is listed for `recuperare`; `recuperazione` exists but is
  specialist.

#### Russian

- 40 of 120 entries annotated, one derived noun per verb.
- `превосходство` is the usual noun for `превосходить` but means superiority,
  not transcendence. `обязательство` means obligation, not the act of obliging.
  `ссылка` means reference or link, not the act of referring in every context.
- `подчёркивание` keeps the ё spelling used by the headword family.

#### Greek

- 40 of 122 entries annotated, one derived noun per verb.
- `κατηγόρημα` is the predicate noun of `κατηγορώ` in the logical sense; the
  same verb also means "accuse".
- `ηγεμονία` is hegemony, not a -ποίηση formation. `σύγχυση` is the standard
  noun for `συγχέω`.

### C2 English curated pass


94 English C2 entries received 118 links where WordNet had no usable relative:
Latin and Greek terms such as `mimesis → mimetic`, `noumenon → noumenal`,
`anaphora → anaphoric`, and verbs such as `reify → reification`,
`bequeath → bequest`, `deride → derision`. Every term in this pass was checked
against WordNet before it was written. The 159 English C2 entries still without
a field are mostly fixed Latin phrases (`prima facie`, `reductio ad absurdum`),
proper or technical terms, and adjectives whose only relatives are too distant
to count as a word family.

### C2 non-English adjective pass


150 adjective entries received one derived noun and one adverb. The forms are
standard morphology, but several are formal or technical:

- French `abstrusité`, `inimitabilité`, `apodicticité`
- Italian `astrusità`, `inequivocabilità`, `apoditticità`
- Russian `нюансированность`, `аподиктичность`, `гетеродоксальность`
- Greek `πολυπολικότητα`, `κατηγορηματικότητα`, `αποδεικτικότητα`

was not added.

### Gendered forms

- French `moral` → `moraux` (masculine plural) and `préventif` → `préventifs`
  are irregular in spelling but standard; `durable`, `civique`, `chronique`
  and `éthique` are invariable in gender.
- Italian `-ista` adjectives (`ottimista`, `pessimista`, `entusiasta`,
  `egoista`) have `-isti` masculine plural and `-iste` feminine plural.
  Invariable `-e` adjectives repeat the same form.
- Russian uses `plural_form` for the single plural (`устойчивые`), not the
  Romance `masculine_plural`/`feminine_plural` pair.
- Greek now uses all five fields, including the new `neuter_plural`
  (`πολιτικά`, `βιώσιμα`). The forms are nominative only.

## Excluded on purpose

- Multi-word headwords (`future plans`, `inner peace`, `small print`) and all
  idioms: the first pass is single-word lemmas only.
- Entries whose `form` does not match the headword in the existing data (for
  example `en:kindness:adjective`, `en:tact:adjective`, `it:prevention:noun`).
  Annotating those would have required fixing the part of speech and the id,
  which is out of scope for this task.
