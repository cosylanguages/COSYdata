# CEFR Theme Taxonomy (language-neutral core + language modules)

Companion to `CEFR_Unified_Reference.md`. Use this to tag lessons, exercises and tests with themes, and to plan syllabi per CEFR level.

## How to use

- Each sub-theme shows the **level at which it is first introduced systematically** (basic receptive + simple productive use). It is then **recycled and deepened** at higher levels (spiral curriculum). Level `A0` means *Pre-A1*.

- Levels are **typical defaults**, not rules. Each language can override them with a `level_shift` (e.g. Georgian verb screeves later, Russian cases earlier) – see the language-module and profile sections.

- Grammar themes marked *(if present)* only apply when the language has the feature. Optional modules G15–G20 cover phenomena that are not universal.

- Source of defaults: synthesised from the supplied course referentials (French A1–C1, Italian, Greek, Russian) and the CEFR descriptors, plus general teaching practice. Treat as a **starting taxonomy** to be validated per language.

- IDs are stable (`V01.03`, `G06.02`, `S04.01` …) so you can add new themes without renumbering; append new IDs instead of editing old ones.

## 4. Grammar themes (core)


**G01 – Script, sounds & prosody**

- **Pre-A1:** Alphabet/script & writing direction; Sound–letter correspondence & spelling rules
- **A1:** Stress, rhythm & intonation; Special sounds & minimal pairs
- **B1:** Connected speech & reduction
- **B2:** Accent, dialect & register in pronunciation
- **C1:** Prosody for nuance, irony & emphasis


**G02 – Nouns & noun phrases**

- **A1:** Number (singular/plural); Gender / noun classes (if present); Articles & definiteness (if present); Case system (if present); Possession (adjectives, suffixes, genitive)
- **A2:** Countable/uncountable & quantification
- **B1:** Diminutives & augmentatives
- **B2:** Noun derivation & nominalisation
- **C1:** Complex noun phrases & apposition


**G03 – Pronouns & determiners**

- **A1:** Subject/personal pronouns; Demonstratives; Interrogatives
- **A2:** Reflexive pronouns; Direct/indirect object pronouns & clitics
- **B1:** Relative pronouns; Indefinite pronouns & quantifiers
- **B2:** Combined pronouns & placement rules
- **C1:** Emphatic & stylistic pronoun use


**G04 – Adjectives & adverbs**

- **A1:** Agreement & position of adjectives; Basic adverbs (place, time, manner, frequency)
- **A2:** Comparatives & superlatives; Degree & intensifiers
- **B1:** Adverb formation
- **B2:** Participial & complex modifiers
- **C1:** Stylistic placement & nuance


**G05 – Verb forms**

- **A1:** Present tense (regular & key irregulars); Person/number agreement; Copula & auxiliaries
- **A2:** Infinitives & non-finite forms; Participles

**G06 – Tense & aspect**

**G07 – Mood & modality**

**G08 – Voice & valency**

## 13. Level-assignment rules already used in COSYdata

- A0 (Pre-A1) is a real level in COSYdata; A0 and A1 share one folder (`a0_a1`).
- Non-transparent idioms and proverbs are NOT A0–A1: moved to A2 (Russian) or B1 (English, Italian) in the changelog.
- Historical figures and over-level proper names go to A2 or higher.
- Russian aspect pairs are stored as one headword (imperfective) with `aspect_pair`; motion verbs carry `motion_type`.
- French, Italian and Greek: contracted prepositions and partitives are separate entries with `contraction` / `partitive` metadata.
- Country, nationality and day/month names are invariable proper nouns (no plural).
- Spoken-course vocabulary is tagged `domain: general, spoken`; `general` is always listed first.
- From B2, regular plurals, comparatives and superlatives are not stored; irregular ones are.
- Entries need transcription, emoji (or `no_emoji`), and antonyms (or `no_antonym`); synonyms are required from B1.

## 18. Adding a new language (profile schema)

Create one profile per language with these fields, switch on the optional modules it needs, and add overrides:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `script` | script name(s), direction, orthographic depth, extra letters |
| `standards` | standard varieties to teach (e.g. Eastern/Western Armenian; European/Brazilian Portuguese) |
| `gender` | none / 2 / 3 / noun classes |
| `case_count` | number of cases or 'none' |
| `morphology` | isolating / fusional / agglutinative / polysynthetic / templatic |
| `verb_system` | aspect-prominent / tense-prominent / evidential / polypersonal |
| `word_order` | SVO / SOV / VSO / free + notes |
| `phonology_flags` | tone, vowel harmony, mutations, pharyngeals, ejectives |
| `politeness` | T–V, honorific levels, none |
| `reference_docs` | CoE RLD, national standards, textbook syllabi |
| `exams` | exam name, level range, validity, issuer, verified_date |
| `optional_modules` | list of G15–G20 modules to enable |
| `overrides` | list of {subtheme_id, level_shift (+/-), note} |

**Onboarding steps**
1. Fill the profile; mark which core grammar themes are *(if present)* → on/off (e.g. no articles, no gender, no cases).
2. Enable optional modules (tone → G15, classifiers → G16, harmony/mutation → G17, evidentiality → G18, polypersonal verbs → G19, honorifics → G20).
3. Add language-specific vocabulary sub-themes under V22 or new IDs (e.g. V25) if needed (e.g. kinship terms in languages with finer distinctions).
4. Collect official descriptors (CoE RLD, national standards) and exam ladders; store with `source` and `verified_date`.
5. Apply `level_shift` overrides only where evidence exists (textbook syllabus, RLD, exam blueprint).
6. Review with a native-speaker teacher before publishing.

**Examples of overrides**
- Georgian G19 (screeves) +1 level later than default tense themes.
- Russian G02 case themes: nominative/accusative/prepositional at A1, instrumental A2, full paradigm B1 (see module).
- Breton G17 (mutations) introduced from A1 because they affect even basic phrases.

**Quality rules**
- Every theme must be testable by at least one can-do statement.
- Each lesson tag = 1 vocabulary theme + 1 grammar theme + 1 function (S/W) + 1 situation.
- Re-tag recycled content instead of duplicating sub-themes.
